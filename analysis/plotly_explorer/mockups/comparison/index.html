Candidate layouts for the new comparison report. Every chart below is drawn from two real Overview payloads already in the explorer builds, so the clutter, label collisions and empty cases are the real ones.

Page skeleton and control panel

The comparison report gets its own optgroup in Report Type. The Dataset panel becomes two selects; everything on the page re-renders when either changes. The second dataset is loaded as a sidecar exactly like the current dataset switcher does, just without the page reload.

KPI pair row

The existing tiles, but each holds both datasets. A value both datasets share collapses to one line so the eye lands on what differs.

Victory type share

Three ways to put the two donuts next to each other. The slices keep the same order and colour in both, so the same victory type sits in the same place.

Variant 1

Side by side, same colours

Recommended

The straight port of the existing donut. Easy to read each mix on its own; comparing a single type between the two takes a glance back and forth, which is why Variant 3 goes under it on the page.

Variant 2

Nested rings, A inside, B outside

One chart, both mixes. The same type lines up radially so a shift reads as the ring edge sliding. Downside: no room for outside labels on the inner ring, and the inner ring has less area so its small slices nearly vanish.

Variant 3

Share shift bars

Recommended companion

Not a donut, but the only one of the three where "Diplomatic went from 30% to 29%" is a straight read. Lighter shade is A, darker is B, the number at the end is the change in percentage points.

Victory time spread

The two-shade scheme you asked for: each victory colour gets a lighter tint for A and a darker tone for B. Time victories are left out as they are in the current chart (always the turn limit).

Shades

Lighter = A, darker = B, derived from the victory palette

Lightness is nudged ±15–20 points in HSL from the base colour (with floors so Domination and Diplomatic do not disappear into the background). Easy to tune in one function.

Variant 1

Split violins, one per victory type

Recommended

One violin per type: A fills the left half, B the right. The silhouettes share a spine so a shift in the typical turn shows as the two halves disagreeing. Points stay, jittered onto their own half.

Variant 2

Grouped full violins

Two complete violins per type. Each dataset's shape is intact and the beeswarm is cleaner, at the cost of half the width per violin and a less direct shape comparison.

Variant 3

Grouped boxes with the median shift called out

Compact and numeric: median, quartiles and whiskers only, with the median change in turns written above each pair. Loses the shape of the distribution, which is what the violin is for.

Win rate by civilization

The hardest one: 44 civs × 2 datasets × up to 5 victory types. The grouped stacked bars keep every number; the dumbbell and the delta bars give up the victory-type split to make the change itself legible.

Variant 1

Grouped stacked bars, A beside B, stacked by victory type

Your suggestion, with the light/dark pairs. It is complete, and hover still gives the count per block, but at 88 bars the per-type colours are thin slivers and the eye mostly reads the two totals. Sorted by B. Works better as the second chart after the dumbbell than as the headline.

Variant 2

Dumbbell: win rate A → B per civ

Recommended

One row per civ, hollow dot at A, filled dot at B, connector green when the civ gained and red when it lost. Sorted by B so the new ranking reads top to bottom, and the length of each connector is the size of the change. The victory-type split lives in the tooltip and in Variant 1.

Variant 3

Diverging change bars

Just the change in points, biggest gain to biggest loss. The most direct answer to "who moved", but it hides whether a +5 came from 2% or from 30%. Pairs well with the table.

Civilization comparison table

Three takes on the table from your screenshot. Rank change is A − B (places gained, positive is good); every other change is B − A. Green always means the civ did better in B, so for Rank at Game End a negative change is green. Arrows: one for a change above 5% of the column's largest, two above 40%.

Variant 1

Rank plus one chosen metric

Recommended
Metric

Exactly your table, with the metric picked from the sidebar chips (Winrate, Avg Score, Rank at Game End, Tiles Owned, Cities Owned). Stays 7 columns wide, so the colour blocks read at a glance. Sortable by any column like the current stats table.

Variant 2

All metrics at once, A | B | change per group

Everything in one scrollable table with a column group per metric. Complete, and the civ column stays pinned while you scroll sideways, but at 20 columns the green/red blocks turn into noise and the horizontal scroll hides half of it on a laptop.

Variant 3

All metrics, change-first cells

One cell per metric: the change in bold with the A → B values underneath in small type. Fits all metrics without scrolling on a desktop and keeps the colour blocks meaningful. Denser rows, and sorting needs a menu rather than a header click.
